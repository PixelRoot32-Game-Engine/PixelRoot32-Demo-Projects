## How to regenerate

```bash
python games/top_down_city/tools/generate_city_assets.py
```

It overwrites all seven files and refuses to write anything if the layout
self-checks fail. Nothing else needs touching: `CityConstants.h` derives the
world geometry from `tilemaps/city_scene.h`, so changing the map size in the
script is enough to resize the world.

```
generated/
  tilemaps/    one .h/.cpp pair per exported scene
    city_scene.{h,cpp}        the island
    police_station.{h,cpp}    an interior
    corner_shop.{h,cpp}       another one, emitted by the same two functions
                              from the same Interior object
  sprites/     the sprite banks, which belong to no scene
    PlayerSprites.h  VehicleSprites.h  PedestrianSprites.h
```

**One scene, one file pair, one namespace, one `init()`.** The scenes do not
include each other and share no data — separate palettes, separate tileset
pools, separate indices, separate behaviour layers. The only thing they have
in common is the engine's palette-slot *bank*, which is global: `city_scene`
binds seven of the eight background slots and every interior binds the eighth,
to the same sixteen colours. All of them are resident at once, bound before
the first `setScene`, because the bank is global state and a scene swap is not
the place to be rebinding it.

The interiors are emitted by one pair of functions taking one `Interior`
object — the room grid, its legend, whether anybody is on duty in it, and
which floor is deliberately sealed. A third room is an entry in the
generator's `INTERIORS` list; there is no third code path.

## The export format

These files are emitted in the **PixelRoot32 Tilemap Editor's scene export
format**. `gameplay/room_screen/src/assets/` holds an editor-produced example
of the same format; this one comes from a generator instead, because a 128×128
city is not something anyone is going to draw by hand.

| File | Contents |
|------|----------|
| `tilemaps/city_scene.h` | Seven slot palettes, `PALETTE_MAPPING`, map dimensions, spawn tile, the door cell of every building that can be entered, the three `TileMap4bpp` externs, dense `TILE_BEHAVIOR_LAYER_*` arrays with `behavior_layers[]`, the district chunk grid and the minimap swatch tables |
| `tilemaps/city_scene.cpp` | One tileset data pool and `Sprite4bpp` table per layer, a second `Sprite4bpp` table for Items and Details holding the after-dark form, the index and per-cell palette-slot arrays, `init()` and `setNightArt()` |
| `tilemaps/<room>.h` | The eighth slot palette, the room's dimensions, its exit and spawn cells, its officer posts and its counter cell if it has either, two `TileMap4bpp` externs and its own two behaviour layers |
| `tilemaps/<room>.cpp` | The room's own two tileset pools, its indices and palette slots, and `init()`. The pools are the whole interior tileset rather than the subset this room indexes, so a tile index means the same thing in every room |
| `sprites/PlayerSprites.h` | 3 facings × 3 walk frames, the sprite-bank palette and the `ink::` colour handles |

### Three layers

| Layer | Tiles | Slot(s) | Contents | Collision |
|-------|-------|---------|----------|-----------|
| `background` | 77 | nature, urban | Water, sand, grass (plain and `_B` variants) and their 15-mask shore/rim/edge transitions, dirt, roads, lane markings, crosswalks, parking, a manhole, plaza, sidewalk and its 15 kerb variants | `TILE_SOLID` on water, tested whole-tile |
| `items` | 110 | vegetation, furniture, cars, buildings A/B/C | Trees, bushes, hedges, palms, a flower bed; lamps, traffic lights, benches, fences, hydrants, bins, signs, rocks, fountains, parasols, loungers, beach balls; seven car colours in two headings; and the multi-tile stamps cut into tiles: a pool, a truck and sixteen buildings | `TILE_SOLID` on every non-empty cell, tested **per pixel** with erosion |
| `details` | 50 | vegetation, furniture, cars | Every prop's cast shadow (`*_SH`, plus `*_SPILL_SH` for the cell to the right of a tree, bush or fountain), grass tufts, flowers, shells, pebbles | none, ever |

**Why the split matters.** `isWorldPixelSolid` decodes the tile's 4bpp bitmap
and blocks only where a pixel is opaque. That is only useful if props are drawn
on a transparent surround — with the terrain baked into the tile, as a
single-layer map forces, a tree is solid across its whole 16×16 cell and
per-pixel collision degenerates into whole-tile collision. The generator
asserts every prop tile has a cut-out for exactly this reason.

### Eight palette slots

A cell picks its palette through `paletteIndices` (bits 0-2, so the engine's
bank of eight is the ceiling and this scene uses all of it), so a layer is not
tied to one palette: Background switches between the natural and the urban
ramp per cell, Items between vegetation, furniture, cars and three building
sets. One slot per art module:

| Slot | Name | Module | Used by |
|------|------|--------|---------|
| 0 | `NATURE` | `art_nature.py` | water, sand, grass, dirt and their transitions |
| 1 | `URBAN` | `art_urban.py` | roads, markings, crosswalks, parking, manhole, sidewalk, kerbs, plaza |
| 2 | `VEGETATION` | `art_vegetation.py` | trees, bushes, hedges, palms, flower bed, their shadows, tufts, flowers |
| 3 | `FURNITURE` | `art_furniture.py` | lamps, traffic lights, benches, fences, hydrants, bins, signs, rocks, fountain, beach props, pool, their shadows, shells, pebbles |
| 4 | `CARS` | `art_cars.py` | *no tiles.* Cars became actors in stage 2; the slot survives as the palette their sprites are drawn with |
| 5 | `BUILDINGS_A` | `art_buildings_a.py` | office, hall, three blocks, kiosk |
| 6 | `BUILDINGS_B` | `art_buildings_b.py` | bar, blue shop, glass tower, police station, teal house |
| 7 | `BUILDINGS_C` | `art_buildings_c.py` | red houses, beige house, shop with windows |

That is 120 usable colours where a single palette gives 15 — 116 of them
surfaces, because four are lights. Slots 3, 5, 6 and 7 each reserve one entry
(`LAMP_LIT`, `WINDOW_LIT`) that the daylight art never draws and the day/night
tint never darkens, which is what lets a window be lit at midnight rather than
merely blue. The generator refuses to emit a scene that breaks either half of
that. See **The hour it looks like** in the demo README. The player, the
cars and the pedestrians draw through the **sprite** bank instead, which the
background slots never touch: slot 0 is the player's palette (and the global
sprite palette, which is what the HUD's `ink::` aliases resolve through), slot
1 the cars', slots 2–5 the four pedestrian recolours. Hence also the minimap
swatch tables, which pre-match every tile to a player-palette entry so the
overlay can be drawn without switching banks.

`PedestrianSprites.h` carries no walk frames on purpose. A pedestrian draws the
**player's** frames through its own palette slot, so four visibly different
people cost four 32-byte palettes rather than four sprite sheets; the squashed
pose is the only pixel data in the file.

## Packing format

Matches the engine's `Sprite4bpp` (`include/graphics/Renderer.h`):

- 16×16 @ 4bpp → 8 bytes per row → 128 bytes per tile.
- Byte **low** nibble = left pixel, high nibble = right pixel:
  `byte[i] = (px[2i+1] << 4) | px[2i]`.
- Pixel value **0 is transparent** — the blitter skips it. Background tiles are
  fully opaque; Items and Details use 0 as their cut-out.
- Entry 0 of every palette slot must stay `0x0000`. The engine packs the 8bpp
  framebuffer value from RGB565 and treats a packed zero as "leave this pixel
  alone", which is what makes the cut-out work.
- Tile **index** 0 in a tilemap means "skip this cell", so every tileset's slot
  0 is a reserved blank — and Background, which has nothing beneath it, never
  references it.

## Flash cost

| Item | Bytes |
|------|-------|
| Tilesets (205 tiles × 128 B) | 26,240 |
| After-dark tiles (the 20 that differ × 128 B) | 2,560 |
| Layer indices (3 × 16,384) | 49,152 |
| Per-cell palette slots (3 × 16,384) | 49,152 |
| Behaviour layers (3 × 16,384) | 49,152 |
| **Total map data** | **173,696** |
| Vehicle sprites (7 colours × 4 headings × 128 B) | 3,584 |
| Vehicle spawn table (24 × 4 B) | 96 |

The after-dark tileset is two more `Sprite4bpp` tables, and only the tiles
whose drawing actually changes get pixel data: every other entry points back
into the daylight pool. Swapping the two is one pointer per layer, so the
lights cost no RAM and nothing per frame.

All of it is `static const` in flash; RAM holds only the three 40-byte
`TileMap4bpp` descriptors. The Details behaviour layer is all zeros and could
be dropped, but it is emitted anyway so `behavior_layers[i]` lines up with
layer `i`, which is what the editor's exporter does.

## Layout parameters

The layout lives in the `City` class. The knobs worth turning:

| Constant | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| `MAP_W`, `MAP_H` | World size in tiles (128×128) |
| `SEED` | Everything random is seeded from this; the same seed always rebuilds the same city |
| `ROAD_BAND` | Street band width: sidewalk, lane, centre line, lane, sidewalk |
| `BLOCK_PITCH` | Distance between street bands, so blocks are `PITCH - BAND` tiles square |
| `H_AVENUES`, `V_STREETS` | Where the bands are cut |
| `PROMENADE_Y` | The coastal road; vertical streets T-junction into it and stop, so no asphalt reaches the open sand |
| `BAND_WATER_MARGIN` | How far inland a street's full five-tile width must be for it to be drawn there. Bands are clipped to the longest run that satisfies it, which is what keeps roads from running off the shore |
| `CAP_WATER_MARGIN` | The same limit for the kerb cap that closes each street end — one tile closer to the sea than the road itself, never touching it |
| `BAND_MIN_RUN` | A clipped band shorter than this is dropped instead of left as an orphan stub |
| `DOWNTOWN_BOTTOM`, `PARK_RIGHT`, `MARINA_RIGHT` | District boundaries, read by `zone_at()`. Each falls on the far edge of a street band, never inside one, so a band is never half gravel path and half asphalt |
| `VEHICLE_COUNT` | How many driveable cars the whole city holds (24). A budget, not a density: every one of them is a simulated actor |
| `VEHICLE_MIN_GAP` | Tiles between two parked cars, so a street reads as a car here and there rather than a rank |

`blocks()` is the dispatch point: it walks every rectangle the street grid
leaves behind and hands it to the treatment for its district. Adding a district
means adding a branch there and a `_<name>_block` method.

## Layout self-checks

`validate()` runs on every generation and **raises** rather than warns, so a
broken layout can never reach the committed files. Each check is there because
the defect it looks for shipped once:

| Check | The defect it caught |
|-------|----------------------|
| No Background cell uses tile index 0 | The renderer skips index 0, so those cells would be holes in the terrain |
| Background tiles are opaque; prop tiles are not | An opaque prop is solid across its whole cell in every mode, which silently disables per-pixel collision |
| No paved tile touches water | Streets running off the shore into the sea |
| Junction boxes are bare carriageway with four kerb corners | A crossing band's kerb columns cutting brick strips across the other band's asphalt |
| No centre line inside a junction box | Two crossing dashed lines meeting in the middle |
| No street tile overwritten after being paved | A block rectangle overrunning its bounds and erasing part of the coastal road |
| No prop standing in a carriageway | A park bench dropped onto the gravel path it was meant to sit beside |
| Every vehicle spawn is a free kerbside carriageway cell, and unique | A car welded into a lamp post — or into another car — on the first frame |
| No collision flag on the Details layer | Decoration silently becoming a wall |
| Every district reachable from the spawn | A district sealed off by scenery |
| **Every** contract payphone is pedestrian-legal, off the carriageway, and in the spawn's own foot-reachable component | A chapter the player can see on the minimap and can never walk to — a job that does not exist. One loop runs the rule over every row of `CONTRACT_PHONES`, so chapter 2's phone cannot drift from chapter 1's |
| Every payphone is at least one block from every courier drop, from the weapon pickup and from the other payphone | Two rings drawn on the same corner, which the player cannot tell apart and so cannot choose between — worst of all two payphones, which would be one marker handing out a different chapter each time it is rung |
| Both chapters have a phone to be handed out at | A `CONTRACT_PHONES` shorter than the story, which the running game shows as a chapter that never rings |
| The chapter 2 payphone is at least one block from the station door | A hit handed out on the doormat of the building it sends you into — the armed walk to the station is the mission |
| `BOOST_VEHICLE_INDEX` is in range, in a different district from the phone, has pavement beside it, and is not the car nearest the spawn | A chapter 1 finished by turning round — or one asking for a car already parked where the teaching pistol is |
| `BOOST_DROP_INDEX` is in range and in a different district from the boost car | A driving mission with no drive in it |
| `HIT_OFFICER_INDEX` is in range of the station's `NUM_OFFICERS` | A read past the end of the room's own table — a target standing wherever the next struct's bytes say, which looks like a placement bug for a week |
| The station's room plan holds at least two officer posts | “Walk past the others”, which is the whole shape of chapter 2, becoming aspirational: one officer in the room makes the hit a doorway and a trigger pull |
| The marked officer's post can be walked to from the station's exit mat | A chapter that cannot be finished at all — and the mark is the post chosen for being furthest from the mat, so it is the one most likely to end up behind the desk or inside the holding cell |
| `HIT_OFFICER_STEPS_IN` is the emitted flood-fill distance, is at least the straight line to the same post, and is not zero | The indoor half of chapter 2's clock priced from a straight line instead of a walk. The mark is *selected* for being the longest walk in the room, so the approximation is worst exactly where it would be used — today 18 steps against 16, absorbed by `allowanceSteps`' margin, which is the margin spent on arithmetic rather than on the player. Zero would mean the mark stands on the mat: a hit finished from the doorway, and a lobby nobody enters |
| A lit palette entry is drawn by the after-dark art and by nothing else | Drawn by neither, the lights come on over nothing; drawn by the daylight art too, and whatever else uses that entry glows at noon — the tint leaves a lit entry alone everywhere, not just after dark |
| The city holds enough lamps and lit windows to see | A perfect after-dark tileset over a layout that stopped placing the props carrying the light |

The junction check decides whether a crossing exists by reading the four
approaches, one tile **outside** the box — never the box itself. Reading the box
made the check vacuous: the defect it hunts is a corrupted box, and a corrupted
box was simply dismissed as "no crossing here". Every check above was verified
by re-introducing its defect and confirming it fires.
